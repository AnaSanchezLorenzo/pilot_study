# pilot_study
Repository for pilot study analysis

# Data description

The aim of this pilot study is to compare three microsampling devices profiles (Whatman paper, Mitra and Capitainer) with reference liquid samples, plasma and whole blood, in an untargeted LCMS analysis. The final objective is to determine which device perform a similar profile to plasma, gold standard sample, generating less artefacts. For this, there will be a qualitative comparison (number of features, metabolites annotated) and a semi-quantitative comparison (intensity and statistical differences).

Samples of study

The samples used in this study were venous blood samples collected from 10 healthy individuals ( 5 men and 5 women) in EDTA tubes. Part of this blood was used as whole blood sample, part was used for plasma extraction and part was spiked in devices to simulate sample collection through fingerprick.

Sample preparation and acquisition

Samples were treated H2O, for sample extraction from device and blood hidrolisis, and IPA for protein precipitation and lipids extraction. Then, extracts were analysed in a UPLC-ESI-QTOF system (Synapt-G2S) (Waters Corporation) using resolution mode for MS1 and FastDDA mode for MS2 data.
This batch of samples only represents the reversed-phase positive ionisation mode data acquisition. 
Conventional untargeted reversed-phase lipid profiling was performed using a UPLC ACQUITY™ BEH C8 (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.7 µm) column with a flow rate of 0.6 mL/min over 13.25 minutes and 2 µL injection volume.
