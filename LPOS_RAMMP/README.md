# pilot_study
Repository for pilot study analysis (RAMMP)

# Data description

Rapid Microbore Metabolic Profiling (RAMMP) methods were developed to provide ultra-fast liquid chromatography analysis cycles of up to five minutes while maintaining high retention time repeatability. 
This is achieved by scaling down column, tubing, and probe dimensions, reducing particle size, and decreasing flow rates. 

This study evaluates the feasibility of using RAMMP method for metabolic analysis of blood microsampling devices and compares its performance with a conventional UPLC-MS approach, as well as with reference liquid samples, including plasma and whole blood.


Samples of study

The samples used in this study were venous blood samples collected from 10 healthy individuals ( 5 men and 5 women) in EDTA tubes. Part of this blood was used as whole blood sample, part was used for plasma extraction and part was spiked in devices to simulate sample collection through fingerprick.

Sample preparation and acquisition

Samples were treated H2O, for sample extraction from device and blood hidrolisis, and IPA for protein precipitation and lipids extraction. Then, extracts were analysed in a UPLC-ESI-QTOF system (Synapt-G2S) (Waters Corporation) using resolution mode for MS1 and FastDDA mode for MS2 data.
This batch of samples only represents the reversed-phase positive ionisation mode data acquisition. 

The convention in-house method was scaled down using a UPLC ACQUITY™ BEH C8 (1.0 × 50 mm, 1.7 µm) column, with a reduced flow rate of 0.3 mL/min over 3.7 minutes and 1 µL injection volume. 
Blood and plasma samples from 10 healthy volunteers were provided by the HUMAN Doctoral Network. 
Data acquisition was conducted on a Synapt G2-S system in positive ionisation mode.
